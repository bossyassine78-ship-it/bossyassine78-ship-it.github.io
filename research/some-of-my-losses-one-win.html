PERSONAL RESEARCH / FIELD NOTES / 001

Some of my losses.
One of my wins.

What survives independent review is more important than the severity I first imagined. These are my lessons from researching identity, authorization and system state.

Disclosure note. This is a reflection on my work, not a published version of any private submission. No production endpoints, secret values, reproduction steps from real targets or nonpublic investigation details are included. Technical demonstrations are available in my separate original local lab.

Loss 01 / The observation I couldn't replay

I saw a result that looked interesting, but circumstances changed before I captured a complete, independently reproducible proof. The difference between what I saw and what I can demonstrate became painfully clear.

Lesson: save the starting state, exact request, response, sequence and date before extending the attack hypothesis. A spectacular conclusion with a missing reproduction is weak evidence.

Loss 02 / A hypothesis with a missing prerequisite

A trust-boundary assumption can look wrong without establishing a practical exploit. An outside attacker still needs a documented path to the necessary capability, and a reviewer is right to ask where that capability comes from.

Lesson: distinguish the observed security decision, the assumptions necessary for exploitation, and actual confirmed impact.

Loss 03 / The scope boundary

Even when a technical mechanism is interesting, the program's authorized scope matters independently from impact. I learned to map affected assets and intermediaries before pursuing a chained workflow.

Lesson: document the scope for each step, not only for the final destination.

The win / Reproduction, review, retest

One finding made it through independent review and a follow-up validation cycle. I learned what it means to close the loop: show an observable behavior, make it reviewable, and verify the outcome afterward.

I am not publishing proprietary report data or claiming that other findings were accepted. A private report can remain private and still change how I work.
